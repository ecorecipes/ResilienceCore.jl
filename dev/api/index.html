API Reference

Operators

ResilienceCore.LinearisedDynamics — Type
LinearisedDynamics{T<:AbstractTimeSemantics}

A matrix together with what it means for the dynamics, in continuous (ContinuousTime) or discrete (DiscreteTime) time. Subtypes:

  • EquilibriumJacobian: perturbations about an equilibrium or fixed point, which decay to zero when it is stable;
  • ProjectionOperator: density-independent growth, where perturbations of the population structure decay relative to the dominant mode.
source
ResilienceCore.EquilibriumJacobian — Type
EquilibriumJacobian{T}(J, equilibrium = Float64[])
ContinuousJacobian(J, equilibrium = Float64[])
DiscreteJacobian(J, equilibrium = Float64[])

The Jacobian J of a non-linear model at an equilibrium (ContinuousTime, dx/dt = f(x)) or a fixed point (DiscreteTime, x′ = f(x)). Small perturbations evolve as exp(J t) or Jᵗ and decay to zero when the point is stable. equilibrium (the state at the point, e.g. species abundances) is needed only for the median metrics, whose default pulses are proportional to it.

source
ResilienceCore.ProjectionOperator — Type
ProjectionOperator{T}(A; weights = ones(n))
ProjectionMatrix(A; weights)    # DiscreteTime: n(t + 1) = A n(t)
Generator(Q; weights)           # ContinuousTime: dn/dt = Q n

A density-independent linear model: a non-negative projection matrix (matrix projection model, or a discretised integral projection kernel) or a generator with non-negative off-diagonal entries (finite-state continuous-time model). There is no equilibrium; the population grows at the rate growth_rate, and the stability metrics describe how perturbations of its structure decay relative to that growth.

weights are the mesh weights of a discretised continuous state (an IPM's bin widths), so that norms approximate integrals and the metrics do not depend on the mesh. Use ones for a matrix model.

source
ResilienceCore.PeriodicOperator — Type
PeriodicOperator(parts)

A periodic (e.g. seasonal) discrete-time model: the operators parts[1], parts[2], … are applied in turn, all of the same kind. Its metrics are those of the period map monodromy, expressed per step.

source
ResilienceCore.MarkovKernel — Type
MarkovKernel(P)

A row-stochastic transition matrix (P[i, j] is the probability of moving from i to j), which moves distributions, not abundances: its dominant eigenvalue is always 1, so growth-based and equilibrium-based metrics do not apply. See resilience, mixing_time.

source

Asymptotic metrics

ResilienceCore.growth_rate — Function
growth_rate(op::ProjectionOperator)
growth_rate(p::PeriodicOperator{<:ProjectionOperator})

The asymptotic growth rate of a density-independent model: the dominant eigenvalue λ of a projection matrix (per step), the spectral abscissa of a generator (per unit time), or λ(period map)^(1/period) for a periodic model. Not defined for equilibria, which do not grow.

source
ResilienceCore.resilience — Function
resilience(op)

The asymptotic rate at which perturbations die out (larger is more resilient), per unit time or per step:

  • continuous-time equilibrium: −Re λ₁(J) (Pimm & Lawton);
  • discrete-time fixed point: −ln ρ(J);
  • projection matrix: ln(λ₁/|λ₂|), the log damping ratio, the rate of convergence to the stable structure (Caswell 2001);
  • generator: Re λ₁ − Re λ₂;
  • periodic model: the rate of its period map divided by the period;
  • Markov kernel: −ln |λ₂|, the rate of convergence to the stationary distribution.

For an equilibrium, a negative value means it is unstable.

source
ResilienceCore.isstable — Function
isstable(op)

Whether an equilibrium or fixed point is locally asymptotically stable (resilience(op) > 0). Not defined for density-independent models, whose populations grow or decline.

source

Transient metrics

ResilienceCore.reactivity — Function
reactivity(op; p = 2 for equilibria, 1 for projections)

The largest initial rate at which a perturbation can grow, per unit time or per step:

  • continuous time: the logarithmic norm μ_p of J (for p = 2, the largest eigenvalue of (J + Jᵀ)/2; Neubert & Caswell 1997), or of Q − s I for a generator;
  • discrete time: ln ‖J‖_p, or ln ‖A/λ‖_p for a projection matrix (Stott et al. 2011 use the 1-norm, the total population, without the logarithm).

Positive means some perturbation grows at first (the system is reactive).

source
ResilienceCore.propagator — Function
propagator(op, t; p)

The standardised propagator: exp(M t) in continuous time, Mᵗ (integer t) in discrete time, with M the operator relative to its asymptotic growth, in weighted coordinates.

source
ResilienceCore.amplification_envelope — Function
amplification_envelope(op, t; p)

‖propagator(op, t)‖_p: the largest factor by which any perturbation is amplified at time t (Neubert & Caswell 1997), relative to asymptotic growth for a projection.

source
ResilienceCore.max_amplification — Function
max_amplification(op; p, dt = 0.1, tmax = 1e4, settle = 1e-8)

(Amax, tmax): the maximum of amplification_envelope and when it is reached, on a time grid of step dt (continuous time) or over integer steps (discrete time). If the operator is not reactive in the chosen norm, the envelope never exceeds its initial value of 1 and the result is (1.0, 0). Otherwise the scan stops when the propagator is within settle of its limit (zero for an equilibrium; the dominant-mode projector for a projection, whose norm is the asymptotic amplification) or at tmax.

source

Median metrics

ResilienceCore.pulse_covariance — Function
pulse_covariance(B)
pulse_covariance(op::EquilibriumJacobian)

The covariance Diagonal(B.^2) of pulses proportional to the equilibrium state B (Arnoldi et al. 2018; Domínguez-García et al. 2019).

source
ResilienceCore.median_displacement — Function
median_displacement(op, C, t)

The median size of a pulse of covariance C after time t relative to its initial size, sqrt(Tr(C Φᵀ Φ) / Tr C) with Φ the propagator (exp(J t), or Jᵗ for integer t).

source
ResilienceCore.median_reactivity — Function
median_reactivity(op, C = pulse_covariance(op))

The median initial rate of displacement: Tr(C J) / Tr C in continuous time, and ln median_displacement(op, C, 1) per step in discrete time (the two agree when J_d = exp(J Δt) and Δt → 0).

source
ResilienceCore.median_max_amplification — Function
median_max_amplification(op, C = pulse_covariance(op); dt = 0.1, threshold = 0.005, tmax)

(MAmax, Mtmax): the maximum of median_displacement and when it is reached, scanning until the displacement falls below threshold (time grid dt in continuous time, integer steps in discrete time). The scan does not stop when the displacement first falls: a median pulse can shrink and then grow.

source
ResilienceCore.median_resilience — Function
median_resilience(op, C = pulse_covariance(op); threshold = 0.005, dt = 0.1)

The median return rate −ln(median_displacement²) / (2t), at the first time on the grid (step dt, or integer steps) at which the displacement is below threshold. NaN if the equilibrium is unstable.

source

Press and stochastic perturbations

ResilienceCore.sensitivity_matrix — Function
sensitivity_matrix(op::EquilibriumJacobian)

The change in the equilibrium per unit sustained press on each variable (Bender et al. 1984): −J⁻¹ for a flow and (I − J)⁻¹ for a map. The two agree for J_d = I + Δt J (with the press scaled by Δt).

source
ResilienceCore.press_sensitivity — Function
press_sensitivity(op)

Community sensitivity Σᵢ |Σⱼ sᵢⱼ| of the sensitivity_matrix (Domínguez-García et al. 2019, SI eq. 15).

source
ResilienceCore.total_sensitivity — Function
total_sensitivity(op)

Σᵢⱼ |sᵢⱼ|, the summed absolute entries of the sensitivity_matrix.

source
ResilienceCore.stochastic_invariability — Function
stochastic_invariability(op::EquilibriumJacobian; dense_max)

Intrinsic stochastic invariability (Arnoldi et al. 2016): the inverse of the largest stationary variance response to white noise.

  • Flow: 1 / (2‖Ĵ⁻¹‖₂) with Ĵ = J ⊗ I + I ⊗ J, from the Lyapunov equation. For n > dense_max it is found by power iteration on Sylvester solves, in O(n²) memory.
  • Map: σ_min(I − J ⊗ J) / 2 per step, from the discrete Lyapunov (Stein) equation, which equals the flow value times Δt for J_d = exp(J Δt), Δt → 0. Dense only; n must be at most dense_max.
source
ResilienceCore.growth_sensitivity — Function
growth_sensitivity(op::ProjectionOperator)

The sensitivity of the growth rate to each matrix entry, ∂λ/∂aᵢⱼ = vᵢ wⱼ / ⟨v, w⟩, with w the stable structure and v the reproductive values (Caswell 2001). For a generator, the same for the spectral abscissa.

source
ResilienceCore.elasticity — Function
elasticity(op::ProjectionMatrix)

Proportional sensitivities eᵢⱼ = (aᵢⱼ/λ) ∂λ/∂aᵢⱼ; they sum to one.

source

Community interface and simulation experiments

A model package implements state, present_species, with_mortality, without_species and jacobian for its community type; the experiments then run on it unchanged.

ResilienceCore.state — Function
state(c)

The abundance of every species the community started with, in a fixed order, zero for those absent. Part of the community interface.

source
ResilienceCore.present_species — Function
present_species(c)

Indices (into state) of the species present. Part of the community interface.

source
ResilienceCore.with_mortality — Function
with_mortality(c, factors; newton = true, steady = nothing)

The community after setting every species' mortality to factors times its baseline (a vector over all species, in the order of state) and moving it to the new steady state from its current state. newton allows a model to try a Newton step first. steady passes model-specific steady-state settings (nothing: the model's default for perturbations). Part of the community interface.

without_species(c, i; steady = nothing)

The community after removing species i and moving to the new steady state. Part of the community interface.

source
ResilienceCore.jacobian — Function
jacobian(c)

The Jacobian of a community model at its current state, over the species present. Model packages add methods for their own types.

source
ResilienceCore.richness — Function
richness(c)

The number of species present.

source
ResilienceCore.abundances — Function
abundances(c)

The abundances of the species present, state(c)[present_species(c)].

source
ResilienceCore.PerturbationOptions — Type
PerturbationOptions(; press = 0.1, global_start = 1.001, step = 0.1, coarse = 10,
                      max_steps = 2000, sequences = 10, steady = nothing)

Settings of the simulated perturbation experiments:

  • press: the relative size of the mortality press for resistance and sensitivity (10%).
  • global_start, step: the first global mortality multiplier and the grid step of the tolerance searches.
  • coarse: the first jump of the tolerance search, in grid steps. It doubles while no species dies and halves when one does, down to single grid steps.
  • max_steps: the largest number of grid steps tried.
  • sequences: the number of random removal sequences for extinction_tolerance.
  • steady: model-specific steady-state settings, passed through (nothing uses the model's own default for perturbations).

The defaults follow Domínguez-García et al. (2019).

source
ResilienceCore.global_mortality_tolerance — Function
global_mortality_tolerance(c; options = PerturbationOptions())

The mortality multiplier global_start + step k (k = 0, 1, …), applied to every species, at which the first extinction occurs (Wootton & Stouffer 2016). NaN if none occurs within max_steps.

source
ResilienceCore.local_mortality_tolerances — Function
local_mortality_tolerances(c; options = PerturbationOptions())

For each species present, the smallest relative increase step k (k = 1, 2, …) in its own mortality that causes any extinction, in the order of present_species.

source
ResilienceCore.press_response — Function
press_response(c, factors; options = PerturbationOptions(), newton = true)

Set mortality multipliers factors (a vector over all species), move to the new steady state, and return (relative_change, l1_change, extinctions). These are (B₀ − B′)/B₀ for total abundance, the L1 norm of the species-wise change divided by B₀, and the number of species lost.

source
ResilienceCore.removal_responses — Function
removal_responses(c; options = PerturbationOptions())

Remove each species present in turn and move to the new steady state. Returns a vector of (survivors, relative, l1): the number of species left (at most N − 1) and the relative and L1 changes in abundance, as in press_response.

source
ResilienceCore.extinction_tolerance — Function
extinction_tolerance([rng], c; options = PerturbationOptions())

Robustness (Dunne et al. 2002): the number of random primary removals, each followed by a return to steady state, needed before at most half of the original species remain, averaged over options.sequences random sequences.

source

Operators from models

linearise turns a model into the operator its metrics need. A community gives the Jacobian at its current state. A SciML ODEProblem, SteadyStateProblem or DiscreteProblem gives the Jacobian at an equilibrium or fixed point. Every ecorecipes package lowers its models to these with to_ode_problem or to_discrete_problem, so this route reaches any model with a steady state. Density-independent matrix and integral projection models give a projection operator, with mesh weights for an IPM. The model-specific methods are package extensions, loaded with SciMLBase (plus ForwardDiff for automatic differentiation), MatrixProjectionModels or IntegralProjectionModels.

using ResilienceCore, OrdinaryDiffEq          # OrdinaryDiffEq loads SciMLBase and ForwardDiff

lv(u, p, t) = p.r .* u .* (1 .- p.A * u)      # Lotka–Volterra competition
p = (r = [1.0, 0.7], A = [1.0 0.3; 0.4 1.0])
ustar = p.A \ ones(2)
J = linearise(ODEProblem(lv, ustar, (0.0, 1.0), p))   # a ContinuousJacobian
resilience(J), reactivity(J)
ResilienceCore.linearise — Function
linearise(prob::SciMLBase.ODEProblem, u = prob.u0; p = prob.p, t = first(prob.tspan),
          tol = 1e-8, check = true, equilibrium = u)              -> ContinuousJacobian
linearise(prob::SciMLBase.SteadyStateProblem, u = prob.u0; ...)  -> ContinuousJacobian
linearise(prob::SciMLBase.DiscreteProblem, u = prob.u0; ...)     -> DiscreteJacobian

The Jacobian of a SciML model at an equilibrium (f(u) = 0) or a fixed point of a map (f(u) = u), as an EquilibriumJacobian. This is the universal route from the ecorecipes packages: each lowers its models to SciML with to_ode_problem or to_discrete_problem.

  • f may be in place or out of place.
  • If the problem's function carries an analytic jac, it is used. Otherwise the Jacobian is computed by automatic differentiation, which needs ForwardDiff to be loaded (using ForwardDiff; OrdinaryDiffEq loads it).
  • u must be an equilibrium or fixed point: the residual ‖f(u)‖∞ (or ‖f(u) − u‖∞) must be at most tol · max(1, ‖u‖∞), otherwise an ArgumentError is thrown. Find u first, for example with a SteadyStateProblem, or pass check = false.
  • equilibrium is stored with the Jacobian for the median metrics, whose default pulses are proportional to it. Pass Float64[] when the state is not a vector of abundances.

Available when SciMLBase is loaded (extension ResilienceCoreSciMLBaseExt).

linearise(m::MatrixProjectionModels.MatrixProjectionModel)   -> ProjectionMatrix
linearise(prob::MatrixProjectionModels.MPMProblem)           -> ProjectionMatrix
linearise(prob::IntegralProjectionModels.IPMProblem)         -> ProjectionMatrix

The density-independent projection operator of a matrix or integral projection model, as a ProjectionOperator. For an IPM the kernel is discretised with materialize and the operator carries the mesh weights of its domain (see mesh_weights), so that its metrics approximate those of the integral operator and converge as the mesh is refined.

Only density-independent, deterministic problems have a single projection operator. A density-dependent model is linearised at a fixed point instead, through its SciML DiscreteProblem; a stochastic one has a stochastic growth rate but no single spectrum.

Available when MatrixProjectionModels or IntegralProjectionModels is loaded (extensions ResilienceCoreMatrixProjectionModelsExt and ResilienceCoreIntegralProjectionModelsExt).

source
ResilienceCore.mesh_weights — Function
mesh_weights(domain::ContinuousDomain) -> fill(step_size(domain), n_states(domain))
mesh_weights(domain::DiscreteDomain)   -> ones(n_states(domain))

The weights of the states of a discretised domain from StructuredPopulationCore: the bin width h for each mesh point of a continuous domain (midpoint rule) and one for each level of a discrete domain. Passed as weights to a ProjectionOperator, they make norms and covariances approximate integrals.
